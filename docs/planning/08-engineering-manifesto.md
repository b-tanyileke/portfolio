## Purpose

This manifesto defines the engineering principles that guide how I approach software development, problem solving, and continuous learning.

It is not intended to describe technologies or frameworks.

Instead, it captures the mindset that shapes my work.

As my career develops, these principles may evolve, but they should always reflect thoughtful engineering and professional integrity.

---

## Core Principles

### 1. Solve Real Problems

Technology is a means to an end.
I strive to build solutions that address meaningful problems rather than creating projects solely to demonstrate technical complexity.

---

### 2. Understand Before Building

Before writing code, I seek to understand the problem, the users, and the constraints.
A well-defined problem often leads to a simpler and more effective solution.

---

### 3. Evidence Over Assumptions

Engineering decisions should be supported by experimentation, documentation, testing, or measurable outcomes whenever possible.

---

### 4. Documentation Is Part of the Product

Clear documentation improves maintainability, collaboration, and long-term understanding.

Writing is an engineering skill.

---

### 5. Simplicity Scales

Simple systems are easier to understand, maintain, and improve.
Whenever possible, I prefer straightforward solutions over unnecessary complexity.

---

### 6. Design for Change

Requirements evolve.
Good software should be adaptable without requiring complete redesign.

---

### 7. Learn Continuously

Every project is an opportunity to develop new skills, evaluate different approaches, and improve engineering judgment.

Learning is never considered complete.

---

### 8. Reflect and Improve

Finishing a project is not the end of the engineering process.
Reflection identifies strengths, weaknesses, and opportunities for future improvement.

---

### 9. Share Knowledge

Engineering grows through collaboration and shared learning.

Whenever possible, I aim to communicate ideas clearly and contribute knowledge that may benefit others.

---

### 10. Build With Integrity

Professional engineering requires honesty, curiosity, accountability, and respect for users, collaborators, and future maintainers.

These values guide both technical and non-technical decisions.

---

## Continuous Evolution

This manifesto represents my current perspective on engineering.

As I gain new experiences, these principles may evolve, but the commitment to thoughtful, evidence-based engineering will remain.
